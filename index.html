Someone Dropped from the Room
Follow the protocol based on who dropped
1
Active Participant Drops
Taking Attorney, Defending Attorney, Court Reporter, Deponent/Witness, or Interpreter
Interrupt Now
What to Do:
  • Interrupt the deposition immediately
  • State clearly: "Excuse me, counsel, [participant] has dropped from the call"
  • Recommend going off the record
  • Wait for the participant to rejoin before continuing
  • Alert #video-help if they don't rejoin quickly
🚨 Critical Rule:
Active participants are essential to the deposition. You must interrupt and go off the record immediately when any active participant drops.
2
Non-Active Participant Drops
Observers, non-active opposing counsel, or other attendees
No Action
What to Do:
  • Do NOT interrupt the deposition
  • Non-active participants may leave or join at any time
  • Continue recording normally without disruption
  • Make a note if needed for your own records
✓ Key Point:
Observers and non-active participants do not require any interruption when they drop from the call.
3
YOU (Videographer) Drop
You lost connection to the deposition
Urgent
Protocol to Follow:
1. Stay Calm & Get Ready
  • Have Slack ready on your phone
  • If internet is down: disconnect from WiFi, switch to cellular data
2. Communicate Immediately
  • Alert #video-help via Slack: job number, your name, "I dropped from the room"
  • Use the Video Help Request Bot if available
3. Rejoin & Document
  • Rejoin as quickly as possible using the same meeting link
  • If you can't rejoin, keep #video-help updated via phone
  • Once back: check recording status immediately
  • Alert #video-help if recording was interrupted
  • Document the drop in your post-depo notes
💡 Best Practice
When interrupting for an active participant drop, keep it brief and clear. State who dropped, then immediately recommend going off the record. Let counsel and the Court Reporter determine next steps.
